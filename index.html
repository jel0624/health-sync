Omnitrack
Privacy policy

Your Fitbit Air data, on your own computer.

Omnitrack downloads your sleep, heart rate, activity and glucose data from Google Health to files on your computer, and turns it into a private view of how you spent each day. It runs on your machine. There is no Omnitrack account and no Omnitrack server. (Up to version 0.1 it was called Health Sync.)

What it does

Fitbit data

Every day, copies your Fitbit Air data from the Google Health API into spreadsheet files (CSV) on your computer.

Day view

Combines that data with optional sources you switch on (computer activity, phone location, your calendar) into a labelled timeline: work, sleep, eating, commute and so on.

Voice notes

Optionally transcribes short voice memos on your own computer, so a quick "heading to lunch" helps label the day.

Why it asks for access to your Google Health data

When you connect your Google account, Omnitrack asks for read-only access. It can never change or delete anything in your Google account.

PermissionWhat it is used for
Activity and fitnessSteps, active minutes, calories and exercise sessions, to show when you were moving.
Health metrics and measurementsHeart rate and blood glucose (if a glucose sensor is linked to Google Health), to show your day's physiology next to what you were doing.
SleepSleep sessions and stages, to mark when you were asleep.
ProfileChecked once, right after you sign in, to confirm your Google account has a Google Health profile to read from. It is shown to you and not saved.
The data goes straight from Google to your computer and stays on your own devices. The developer never receives it, and it is not sold, shared or used for advertising. Details are in the privacy policy.

Status

Omnitrack is a personal project in early testing on Windows and macOS. It works with the Fitbit Air and any data you have linked to Google Health.
