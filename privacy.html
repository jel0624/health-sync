Privacy policy

Effective October 1, 2026 (revised the same day: every service is listed below; revised October 7, 2026: the pilot program for invited testers)

  • Omnitrack runs on your own computer. There is no Omnitrack account and no Omnitrack server. (Omnitrack was called Health Sync up to version 0.1; this policy covers both.)
  • Your Google Health data goes directly from Google to your computer and stays on your own devices. The developer never receives it, unless you are an invited tester and join the pilot program.
  • Your data is never sold, never used for advertising and never shared with anyone.

Data Omnitrack reads from Google

With your permission, Omnitrack gets read-only access to your Google Health data. It cannot change or delete anything in your Google account. Depending on what your devices record, it reads:

  • Activity and fitness: steps, distance, calories, active minutes, active zone minutes, heart rate zones, exercise sessions and cardio fitness.
  • Health metrics and measurements: heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, blood oxygen, breathing rate, skin temperature and, if a glucose sensor is linked to Google Health, blood glucose.
  • Sleep: sleep sessions and sleep stages.
  • Profile: read once, right after you sign in, to confirm that your account has Google Health data. It is shown to you and not saved.

It also saves the name of the device or app that recorded each value (for example, your Fitbit Air).

How Omnitrack uses it

Only to show you your own data:

  • to save it as files on your computer that you can open in a spreadsheet;
  • to build a private view of your day on your computer (for example, when you slept, worked, ate or travelled);
  • to learn your personal labels for that view. This learning happens on your computer, and what it learns stays there.

Omnitrack does not use your data for advertising, does not sell it, does not build profiles for anyone else, and does not use it to train any model other than your personal one on your own computer. If you join the pilot program, the data you send is used to improve how Omnitrack labels days.

Where it is stored

On your computer, in the Omnitrack data folder, as ordinary files (on Windows %LOCALAPPDATA%\Omnitrack\data, on a Mac ~/Library/Application Support/Omnitrack/data). Your Google sign-in token is kept in your system's credential store: Windows Credential Manager or the macOS Keychain. The files are not encrypted by Omnitrack and are kept until you delete them, so protect your computer with a password and, ideally, disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on macOS).

Who it is shared with

No one. The developer has no access to your data (unless you join the pilot program and send it), and Omnitrack does not send your Google Health data to any other company or service, with one exception you control: if you connect ChatGPT and turn on health data for the Bloom or Zen coach, your questions to that coach go to OpenAI with your health data. Otherwise the day you ask about goes to OpenAI without any Google Health data (details).

If you turn on phone location, your phone sends its location to your own computer through Tailscale, a private, encrypted network between your own devices that you set up and control. Your Omnitrack pages and settings open only on the computer itself: other devices in that network can send locations (with a secret token) but cannot open your pages.

Google user data policies

The use of information received from the Google Health API will adhere to the Google Health API Developer and User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Omnitrack's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will also adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other data you can choose to add

These sources are optional and are processed on your computer:

  • Computer activity: which apps and windows were in use, recorded locally by ActivityWatch.
  • Phone location: sent from a location app on your phone directly to your own computer.
  • Calendar: downloaded from a calendar link you provide.
  • Voice memos: recordings that your own iCloud account syncs to your computer, then transcribed on your computer. The audio and text are not sent anywhere else, unless you ask ChatGPT about a day (below).

Optional: asking ChatGPT about your day

If you connect ChatGPT in Settings ("Continue with ChatGPT"), you can ask questions about a day from its day page, and ChatGPT answers using your own ChatGPT plan. Nothing is sent until you ask. When you ask, OpenAI receives:

  • your question, your recent conversation with that coach (Spark, Bloom or Zen) and its short notes on your earlier conversations with it (written by ChatGPT, kept on your computer);
  • what you told the coaches about yourself when they got to know you (your job, for example);
  • the text of each goal you add, once, so it becomes something Omnitrack can measure (your progress is worked out on your computer);
  • a text summary of the day you are looking at, and one line for each of the six days before: activities and their times, place names, and, unless you turn them off in Settings, app names, calendar event titles and the text of your voice memos;
  • your health data from Google Health and your glucose sensor (sleep, steps, heart rate, glucose, exercise) only if you turn it on for Bloom or Zen in Settings, and only with your questions to that coach. It is off until you turn it on; Spark never gets it.

Never sent: map coordinates or addresses, window and website titles, and your Google sign-in; and no Google Health data unless you turned it on as above.

OpenAI processes this under your ChatGPT account and OpenAI's privacy policy; Omnitrack asks it not to store the conversation. Your ChatGPT sign-in token is kept on your computer in Windows Credential Manager or the macOS Keychain. The developer receives nothing. Disconnect at any time in Settings, or remove Omnitrack in ChatGPT's settings.

Optional: the pilot program (invited testers only)

Before Omnitrack is released, a few people test it at the developer's invitation. A tester joins on the Pilot page of the app with a personal code, after reading what joining means and ticking "I agree". Nothing is sent before that. A tester can then send bug reports, written feedback and their data, so that the developer can find bugs and compare Omnitrack's labels with the tester's corrections to improve how Omnitrack labels days.

What is sent, only when the tester presses Send, or once a week if they allow it, and only the kinds of data they leave ticked:

  • Google Health data for the days sent: sleep, steps, heart rate, heart rate variability, oxygen saturation, exercise and, if linked, blood glucose;
  • phone location fixes and the places Omnitrack found;
  • computer activity: app names and times, with window titles unless the tester leaves them out;
  • calendar events and voice memo transcripts for those days (each can be left out);
  • Omnitrack's labels, its model's guesses, the questions it asked, and the tester's answers and corrections;
  • with a bug report: what the tester writes, a screenshot if they add one, the page they were on, recent error messages and logs, with passwords, sign-in tokens and private links removed first.

Never sent: sign-in tokens, the calendar link, voice memo audio and Omnitrack conversations.

How it is protected: everything is encrypted on the tester's computer with the developer's public key, so only the developer can open it. It travels through a small relay on Cloudflare (a Worker and its R2 storage), which holds only the encrypted files, the tester's number (for example P03), their size and the time until the developer downloads them, then deletes them. On the developer's computer the data is kept on an encrypted disk.

Who and how long: only the developer reads it, as each tester agreed. It is never sold or shared, never used for advertising, and not used for research studies or publications: only to improve Omnitrack. Google Health data is used only to improve Omnitrack's day labelling, as the Google Health API Developer and User Data Policy allows with the user's consent. Everything a tester sent is deleted when the pilot ends, or earlier when they choose "Leave and delete my data" on the Pilot page.

Omnitrack contacts the services below. None of them receive your Google Health data, except the pilot relay, encrypted, for testers who joined the pilot.

Each of these services handles the requests it receives under its own privacy policy.

Your choices

  • Stop access: remove Omnitrack at myaccount.google.com/permissions. It can then no longer read your Google Health data.
  • Delete your data: uninstall Omnitrack and choose to delete your data (this also removes the sign-in token from Credential Manager or the Keychain), or delete the Omnitrack data folder yourself. Nothing is kept anywhere else, except what a pilot tester sent (next point).
  • Pilot: leave on the Pilot page at any time; "Leave and delete my data" deletes everything you sent, on the relay and on the developer's computer.
  • Turn sources off: each optional source can be left out or switched off in the settings.
  • ChatGPT: Disconnect in Settings (this also signs Omnitrack out at OpenAI), or remove Omnitrack (Health Sync if you connected it before the rename) under Apps in ChatGPT's settings.
