Download Omnitrack

Free, and it runs on your own computer. There is no account to create, and your data stays on your computer. Invited pilot testers can choose to send theirs to the developer (how).

This is a beta (version 0.2.0). The installers are not signed yet, so Windows and macOS will warn you the first time. The steps below show what to click.

Windows

Your computer

Windows 10 (1809 or newer) or 11, 64-bit · installs for you only, no administrator rights

Download for Windows
  1. If your browser says the file is not commonly downloaded: in Edge, point at the download, click ... > Keep, then Show more > Keep anyway. In Chrome, choose Keep (or Download suspicious file).
  2. Open the downloaded Omnitrack-0.2.0-setup.exe. Windows shows "Windows protected your PC". Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Click Install. Setup downloads Python and the libraries Omnitrack needs; this takes a few minutes.
  4. The setup page opens in your browser. Continue there. Later, open Omnitrack from the Start menu.

If Windows says Smart App Control blocked the app, there is no "Run anyway": this beta is not signed, so it cannot be installed while Smart App Control is on (Windows Security > App & browser control > Smart App Control).

Or install with one line (PowerShell) 
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -c "irm https://jel0624.github.io/health-sync/install.ps1 | iex"

Paste it into PowerShell (Start menu > PowerShell). It does the same as the installer above, without the setup window, and adds the same Start-menu entries.

Mac

Your computer

macOS 13 Ventura or newer · Apple Silicon and Intel

Download for Mac
  1. Open the downloaded Omnitrack-0.2.0.pkg. macOS says it could not verify the package. Click Done (or OK).
  2. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down to the message about Omnitrack and click Open Anyway. Confirm with your password.
  3. Follow the installer. It asks for your password once to unpack its files; Omnitrack itself runs as you. It then downloads Python and the libraries (a few minutes).
  4. The setup page opens in your browser. Continue there. Later, open Omnitrack from your Applications folder (in your home folder).
Or install with one line (Terminal) 
curl -fsSL https://jel0624.github.io/health-sync/install.sh | bash

Paste it into Terminal (Applications > Utilities). It does the same as the package, needs no password and shows no security warning.

What you need

  • A Fitbit Air (or another device) whose data is in the Google Health app, and the Google account it belongs to.
  • About 2 GB of free disk space and an internet connection while installing.

Optional, each switched on in the setup page and each with a Test button:

  • Computer activity: ActivityWatch. On Windows the setup page installs it for you. On a Mac, download it from the ActivityWatch site, drag it to Applications and open it, allow it under System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility, and add it under System Settings > General > Login Items so it starts with your Mac.
  • Calendar: the private address of your calendar in iCal format (in Google Calendar: Settings > your calendar > "Secret address in iCal format").
  • Voice memos: the Just Press Record iPhone app (a paid app) saving to iCloud Drive. On Windows you also need iCloud for Windows with iCloud Drive turned on.
  • Phone location: Tailscale on this computer and on your iPhone (same account), and the free Overland iPhone app. The setup page shows a QR code that fills in Overland for you. Your phone only sends its location; your pages open on your computer only.

Why the warnings?

"Windows protected your PC" / "Apple could not verify". Code-signing certificates cost money every year, and this beta is not signed yet. The warning says the publisher is unknown, not that something harmful was found.

"Google hasn't verified this app". When you connect your Google account, Google shows this for apps that have not finished Google's review. Click Advanced, then Go to Omnitrack (unsafe). On the next screen the permission boxes start empty: tick Select all, then Continue. Omnitrack can only read your data; it can never change or delete anything in your Google account.

Omnitrack checks this website once a day for updates (you can turn that off). Updates install with one click and keep your data. What Omnitrack stores and which services it contacts is listed in the privacy policy.

Uninstall

  • Windows: Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Omnitrack > Uninstall (for either installer). Or, in PowerShell: & "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Omnitrack\app\omnitrack.cmd" uninstall
  • Mac: in Terminal, run "$HOME/Library/Application Support/Omnitrack/app/omnitrack" uninstall. It also removes Omnitrack from your Applications folder.
  • Both ask whether to delete your data as well (the default keeps it). To also remove Omnitrack's access to your Google account, visit myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Changes

0.3.0 (October 2026): Pilot release for invited testers: Report a bug and Feedback on every page, sending your data to the developer only if you choose to (encrypted so only he can read it), and automatic updates. New: the Omnimons (chat about your days, goals and an Analysis page), a Journal, a Minimal look and one Settings page.

0.2.0 (October 2026): Health Sync is now called Omnitrack. Installing 0.2.0 (or "Install update" in Health Sync's Settings) replaces Health Sync and keeps everything: your data folder moves to the Omnitrack folder, and your Google sign-in, port and settings stay as they were. Health Sync's background tasks, Start-menu entries or app and its folder are removed. A new welcome page.

0.1.0 (October 2026): first beta for Windows and Mac. Google Health sync and the day view, plus optional computer activity, calendar, voice memos and phone location.
